**Title:** Build a conformance suite: pinned log-densities, gradients, and bijector round-trips

greta has no fast, deterministic way to answer the question "did this change
alter the numbers greta produces?". That question comes up on every TensorFlow
version bump, and it will come up constantly during the op-dictionary and
node-registry rework, where the whole point is to change how the numbers are
computed while keeping them identical.

## What exists today

Seven `test_posteriors_*.R` files: `binomial`, `bivariate_normal`,
`chi_squared`, `lkj`, `standard_uniform`, `wishart`, and `geweke`. Six run on
every PR through `R-CMD-check`; the Geweke checks are gated behind
`GRETA_GEWEKE=true` and run weekly, because they cost roughly 33 minutes for
three samplers.

These are end-to-end statistical checks. They catch gross breakage, but they
are slow, stochastic, and they tell you a sampler is wrong rather than which
log-density or gradient moved.

## What is missing

A layer underneath them that is fast, deterministic, and specific:

1. **Pinned log-densities.** For each distribution family, evaluate the
   log-density at fixed parameter values and fixed data, computed once against
   a known-good greta and stored as package data. greta has 29 distribution
   constructors in `R/probability_distributions.R`.
2. **Pinned gradients.** The same fixtures, comparing the gradient of the
   log-density with respect to the free state. Samplers depend on the gradient,
   not just the value, and a gradient can break while the density stays right.
3. **Bijector round-trips.** For every constrained variable type, check that
   the forward and inverse transforms compose to the identity, and that the
   log-Jacobian adjustment is what it should be. The constructors are in
   `variable_node$create_tf_bijector()` at `R/node_types.R:303`, dispatching to
   the `tf_scalar_*_bijector()` family for the none/low/high/both/mixed
   constraint cases.

## Why it belongs in the test-suite milestone

It was originally scheduled alongside the TensorFlow unpin, on the reasoning
that the unpin was the first change that would need it. The unpin shipped
without it, so it is unbuilt and its first real consumer is now the backend
work. Putting it here rather than there means that milestone does not have to
build its own oracle before it can start.

## Acceptance

- A fixture file of pinned values, generated by a documented script so it can
  be regenerated deliberately rather than by accident.
- Tests that compare against the fixtures within a stated tolerance, and that
  fail loudly with the offending distribution named.
- The suite runs in seconds, on every PR, without the Python stack having to
  produce a posterior.

## Open questions for whoever picks this up

- What tolerance? Too tight and a harmless TFP change breaks CI; too loose and
  it stops catching anything.
- Do the fixtures get regenerated on a deliberate numerical change (e.g. TFP
  0.25 changing how `lkj_correlation()` and `wishart()` sample), and if so, what
  is the review process for accepting a new fixture?
- Is 29 distributions the right granularity, or should this cover a
  representative subset plus every bijector?
